# SYNTHETIC manuscript: blue versus green reminder cards

This fictional experiment is provided solely for testing review-support software. No people,
institutions, or findings described here are real. All materials and complete numerical results
are included below. There are no figures or supplements.

## Introduction

We asked whether the colour of a reminder card changed completion of a fictional checklist.
The prespecified outcome was checklist completion within one simulated session. This report
describes a feasibility exercise; it makes no claim about clinical outcomes or general efficacy.

## Methods

We randomly allocated 120 simulated participants to two parallel groups using a computer-generated
sequence. The blue-card group contained 70 participants and the green-card group contained 70
participants. No participant belonged to both groups. There were no exclusions or withdrawals.
Each participant completed exactly one session. Investigators kept the allocation sequence hidden
until enrolment. The outcome assessor did not see the card colour. Checklist completion was
recorded automatically as a binary outcome. Analysis followed the allocated group. There were
no missing outcomes. We report counts rather than an efficacy test because this exercise was
designed to establish operational feasibility, not demonstrate superiority.

## Results

In the blue-card group, 35 of 70 participants completed the checklist. In the green-card group,
28 of 70 completed it. The database contained one row for each of the 120 allocated participants.
All allocated participants appear in these group totals. The software recorded all outcomes
without an adjudication step. No other analyses were performed.

## Discussion

The observed completion counts describe this fictional sample only. The pilot does not establish
that either colour is better, and no clinical or causal efficacy claim is made. Real-world
implementation would require a new study with an explicit sample-size calculation and a more
representative population. Our main practical observation is that a simulated workflow can
record a binary completion outcome. We did not measure acceptability or participant experience.
