# SYNTHETIC negative control: a descriptive checklist feasibility exercise

## Introduction

This fictional short report asks whether a simulator can record completion of a checklist.
It does not compare treatments, test efficacy, or make clinical recommendations. All observations
are generated by a computer; no real participants or institutions are involved.

## Methods

We generated 120 independent simulation records using a fixed random-number seed of 173. The
prespecified outcome was successful recording of a binary checklist-completion field. We also
describe the proportion of simulated sessions in which the checklist was completed. Every
generated record was included, with no exclusions or missing outcomes. We selected 120 records
as a practical software test batch, not on the basis of statistical power. The simulation
assigned one record to each virtual session; there were no repeated participants or clusters.
No group comparisons, subgroup analyses, or inferential tests were planned or performed.

## Results

All 120 records contained a readable binary outcome, giving a recording success rate of 100%.
The checklist was completed in 60 sessions and was not completed in 60 sessions. Completion was
therefore 50% of the 120 simulated sessions. These counts include every record generated by the
simulator. The dataset contains no other variables and the report contains all numerical results.

## Discussion

This exercise establishes only that the tested software can store and retrieve a binary field
under these simulated conditions. The observed completion proportion has no population-level or
clinical interpretation. It was generated by the simulator and is not evidence of usability,
acceptability, improved care, or treatment effectiveness. We have not tested robustness under
network failure, different software versions, or live clinical workflows. Such tests would be
separate investigations with their own aims and methods. No clinical inference follows here.
