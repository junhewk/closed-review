# Mapping home-oxygen education: a scoping review

Synthetic manuscript. All studies and results are fictional.

## Purpose

Our population–concept–context question was: what educational approaches for adults beginning home oxygen have been described in community care? The purpose was to map approaches and gaps, not determine comparative effectiveness or rank interventions. A public fictional protocol prespecified primary empirical studies and service reports, any design, English-language, 2010–2026. These restrictions reflect translation resources and are limitations, not evidence that other work does not exist.

## Methods

We searched MEDLINE and CINAHL on 1 September 2026 using (home oxygen OR domiciliary oxygen) AND (educat* OR teach* OR train*), adapting subject terms and syntax by source. We also screened reference lists of included reports. Two reviewers independently screened records and full texts, resolving disagreements by discussion. From 90 records we removed 10 duplicates, excluded 50 at abstract and 12 at full text, and included 18 reports about 16 services. Full-text exclusions were inpatient-only context (7), paediatric population (3) and no educational component (2). Reports from the same service were charted together rather than counted as independent services.

## Charting and results

Two reviewers piloted and refined a chart recording setting, participants, educational format, provider and reported outcomes. One charted and the other checked all entries; a change log documented revisions. Nine services described individual demonstrations, five group sessions and two video materials. Four reports were randomized, six observational and eight descriptive service accounts. These report-design totals refer to 18 reports, while format counts refer to 16 services. Outcomes and follow-up definitions varied; we did not pool or compare effects. Formal risk-of-bias appraisal was not undertaken because this review maps provision, not effectiveness. This prevents conclusions about relative benefit.

## Discussion

The map shows where and how education is described, not which format works best. English-only eligibility, two databases and reliance on reporting constrain coverage. There may be unreported services and educational formats. Comparative effectiveness requires an appropriate review question, appraisal and design-sensitive synthesis beyond this map.
