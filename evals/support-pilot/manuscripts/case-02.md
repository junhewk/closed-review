# Ward follow-up calls and 30-day readmission

Synthetic manuscript. All people, institutions and results are invented; no clinical recommendation is intended.

## Objective

To estimate whether offering a nurse follow-up call after discharge reduces 30-day all-cause readmission among adult general-medical inpatients.

## Methods

In a fictional single-centre parallel randomized trial, 200 adults were individually allocated 1:1 to an offered follow-up call or usual discharge. A computer-generated allocation sequence was concealed until consent. Patients were analysed in their assigned groups regardless of whether they answered the call. Each patient contributed one discharge; there was no clustering or matching. Admission records covered all participants for 30 days, with no missing outcomes. The prespecified primary endpoint was any readmission within 30 days; it was binary and unweighted. The effect estimate was the unadjusted risk ratio with the usual log-Wald 95% interval. There were no other primary outcomes or interim tests. No adjusted or causal subgroup analysis was performed. The fictional ethics board approved the study and participants consented.

## Results

Table 1. Allocation and primary outcome; denominators are all randomized participants.

| Outcome | Offered call | Usual discharge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Randomized, n | 100 | 100 |
| Readmitted, n | 20 | 30 |
| Not readmitted, n | 80 | 70 |
| Readmitted, % | 35 | 30 |

The estimated risk ratio was 0.67 (95% CI 0.41–1.09), calculated from 20/100 and 30/100. All 200 patients were included in the primary analysis.

## Discussion

The estimate favours offering a call, but its interval also includes no difference and possible increased risk. These data do not establish a reduction. The single-centre setting and modest sample limit precision and generalizability; no claim about mortality or costs follows. The next study should use a larger, prospectively registered multisite sample. No raw dataset or cited external evidence is supplied with this manuscript.
