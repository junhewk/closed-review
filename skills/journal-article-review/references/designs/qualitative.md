# Qualitative research

## Recognize

Cues: a named qualitative approach, purposive sampling, interviews or observation, themes or
categories as the main result, participant quotations as evidence. A named approach that the methods
never use (for example "phenomenological" with only generic coding) is itself a possible candidate.

## Where it usually breaks

### Coherence of question, approach, and analysis
- Look in: aims, methods, analysis description, how results are framed.
- Signals: the stated approach (grounded theory, phenomenology, ethnography) does not shape sampling,
  data generation, or analysis; analysis labels are mixed without reason (reflexive thematic analysis
  with a fixed codebook and agreement statistics); a "how" or "why" question answered with topic lists.
- Would settle it: a short account of why this approach fits the question and how it shaped each step.
- Proportionate fixes: state the approach accurately, describe the procedure actually used, or relabel
  as qualitative description.

### Sampling and information power
- Look in: recruitment, eligibility, participant table, limitations.
- Signals: no rationale for who was sampled; recruitment only through gatekeepers who may filter
  views; groups the question implies but the sample lacks; saturation stated as a ritual without saying
  how it was judged, or claimed in an approach that does not use it.
- Would settle it: the sampling logic, how sample adequacy was judged for this aim (information power,
  theoretical sampling, or the approach's own criterion), and who declined or was not reached.
- Proportionate fixes: describe the logic and its limits; narrow claims to the people represented.

### Researcher position and reflexivity
- Look in: methods, author information, limitations.
- Signals: interviewers with power over participants (clinicians interviewing their own patients,
  teachers their students); insider or outsider status left unexamined; reflexivity reduced to a
  sentence that bias was minimized.
- Would settle it: who generated and analyzed the data, their relationship to participants, and how
  that position was handled and may have shaped the findings.

### Data generation
- Look in: setting, guide, duration, recording, transcription, language.
- Signals: no interview guide or topic areas; unclear mode or length; translation of data or quotes
  without saying when, by whom, and whether analysis was in the original language.
- Would settle it: the guide as a supplement, the analysis language, and the translation procedure.

### Analytic process
- Look in: analysis section, supplements, figures of themes.
- Signals: "themes emerged" with no account of how codes became themes; themes that are topic
  summaries (one per interview question) rather than patterns of meaning; software named as if it were
  a method; unclear who coded and how differing readings were used.
- Would settle it: a traceable path from data to themes, with one worked example if space allows.

### Evidence for findings
- Look in: results and quotations.
- Signals: quotes that do not show what the theme claims; most quotes from a few participants; no
  contrary or deviant cases; description too thin to judge interpretation, or quotation without analysis.
- Would settle it: quotes linked to participant identifiers, and an account of variation and exceptions.

### Claims and transferability
- Look in: discussion, conclusions, abstract.
- Signals: frequency claims ("most participants") used as findings; causal or population-level claims;
  practice recommendations that go beyond the data; too little context for readers to judge transfer.
- Proportionate fixes: restate claims at the level the design supports; add context.

### Ethics specific to qualitative data
- Signals: quotes or participant tables that could identify people in small communities or rare
  conditions; consent unclear for observation or for reuse of quotations; distress protocols absent
  for sensitive topics.

## Major flaws

Major when the quoted evidence shows them (see the index).

- **incoherent-approach**: the stated approach does not shape sampling, data generation, or
  analysis, or analysis labels are mixed without reason.
- **unexamined-power**: interviewers with power over participants, and the relationship left
  unexamined.
- **unsupported-themes**: quotes that do not show what the theme claims, or themes that are topic
  summaries.
- **claims-beyond-design**: frequency, causal, or population-level claims, or practice
  recommendations that go beyond the data.
- **identifiable-participants**: quotes or participant tables that could identify people, or consent
  unclear for the use made of the data.

## Common false alarms

- Asking for a larger or representative sample, or a power calculation.
- Asking for inter-rater agreement (such as kappa) in reflexive or interpretive analysis. It fits
  coding-reliability approaches only.
- Treating the absence of saturation as a flaw in an approach that does not use saturation.
- Treating member checking as mandatory. It is one option and has its own problems.
- Scoring quality by COREQ or SRQR completeness. They are reporting aids, not appraisal tools.
- Criticizing subjectivity as such, rather than unexamined subjectivity.
- Asking for themes to be counted or percentages reported.

## Approach notes

- Qualitative description: a legitimate low-inference approach; do not fault it for not being theory.
- Thematic analysis: check which kind (coding reliability, codebook or framework, reflexive). Each
  has different quality markers.
- Grounded theory: expect theoretical sampling, constant comparison, memos, and a theory or model as
  the result. Coding alone is not grounded theory.
- Phenomenology: descriptive (bracketing, essential structure) and hermeneutic or IPA (interpretation,
  small homogeneous samples, idiographic depth) differ. Check that the claims match the one named.
- Ethnography: expect prolonged engagement and observation, not interviews alone.
- Content analysis: counting can be appropriate here, unlike in reflexive thematic analysis.
- Framework analysis: a matrix and an a priori framework are expected; check how it was revised.

## Monitoring areas

Coherence of approach; sampling and adequacy; reflexivity; data generation; analytic process;
evidence for findings; claims and transferability; qualitative ethics.
